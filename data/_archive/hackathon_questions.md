>

---

## What gets scored

Each scored 1–5 (OK · Fair · Good · Great · Excellent):

| # | Criterion | What they are asking |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Innovativeness** | How innovative the proposed approach is, relative to existing responses. |
| 2 | **Feasibility** | How realistic the prototype is to build, test, and take further. |
| 3 | **Potential Impact** | How much real-world difference the solution could make if it worked at scale. |

---

## Stage 1 — Get a Closer Look

- Who exactly is affected — a real person, not just "people"?
- What's already been tried, and why hasn't it worked?
- What part of this can we realistically tackle today?

## Stage 2 — Generate Ideas *(Solution Creativity)*

- What's the boring, expected answer — and how do we beat it?
- No limits: what would we build? Now, the smallest version of that?
- Is there an idea from elsewhere we could borrow and adapt?
- Which idea would actually surprise the jury?
- App, platform, service, tool, or business model — which is this, really?

## Stage 3 — Stress-Test Feasibility

- What do we need to build a rough version of this today?
- What's our riskiest assumption — how do we test it today?
- One more week: what would we build next?
- Who's best placed to build, and later demo, each part?

## Stage 4 — Define Impact *(Potential Impact)*

- If this worked, what changes for the people affected?
- How many people could this reach — and how would we know?
- What would it take to work in another city or country?
- What's the one sentence we want the jury to remember?

## Stage 5 — Prep the Pitch

- **Creativity:** what's different, in one sentence?
- **Feasibility:** can we show it, not just tell it?
- **Impact:** who benefits, and roughly how much?
- Have we rehearsed so it doesn't run over **4 minutes**?

---

### Note on the scoring scale

Stage 5 mirrors the three scoring criteria exactly. The pitch is therefore not a
summary of the project — it is three answers, in order, in under four minutes.
